A trip in Bite Book is the container for a hunt. It holds the dates, the hunters, the species, the harvests you log, and the state report you generate at the end. Get the fields right when you create it and the rest of the trip is downhill.

## Start

From the dashboard, tap **Add trip**. You land on a form with five sections.

## Basics

- **Trip name.** What you’ll search for later. Use the season + the species + the party. “Fall black bear — Johnson party.” Don’t use a date — Bite Book already tracks dates.
- **Status.** Defaults to **Planned**. Leave it. Bite Book flips it to Active on day one and to Completed when you wrap it up.

## Dates

- **Start date.** The first day in the field.
- **End date.** The last day. Same day as Start for a single-day hunt.

## Location

- **State** (required). Picks the right harvest log template. If you guide in two states, create one trip per state.
- **City** (optional). Reads on the trip card and on some state forms.
- **Zone or unit** (optional). Required by some states for harvest reports — Colorado, Idaho, and a few others. If you don’t know your zone, leave blank and add it later.
- **County** (optional). A handful of states want this on the report. The trip-detail page tells you if your state needs it.

## Hunt details

- **Activity.** Hunting, fishing, or both. Picks the species dropdown.
- **Species.** Pick one or more. Mixed-bag trips can list everything in one trip — you don’t need separate trips per species.
- **Method.** Rifle, bow, muzzleloader, etc. State reports want this on the harvest log.

## Hunters

You can leave this empty when you create the trip and add hunters later. If you already know who’s coming, type their email or pick from your existing hunter list. They get an invite as soon as you save.

## Notes

Free text. Anything you want to remember — gate codes, who’s bringing what, weather notes from past years on the same property. Doesn’t go on any state form.

Tap **Save**. Your trip lands on the dashboard under **Upcoming**.
